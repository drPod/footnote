# Demo Scenarios — Five Tools, One Family, Five Workflows

**Project:** Hackathon pitch artifact for an AI paraplanner serving wealth-management advisors.
**Thesis:** Existing advisor-AI tools (Jump, Zocks, Salesforce Agentforce FS) see only meeting transcripts and CRM notes. Manual+ChatGPT workflows see whatever the advisor remembers to paste. **OURS** sees the client's full document context — tax returns, brokerage statements, 401(k) statements, life-insurance illustrations, estate documents, RSU grant agreements, life-event documentation — and grounds every recommendation in those primary sources.

The pitch turns on a single question: **show the same task done by all five, side by side, on a realistic family.** The gap should be felt, not argued.

# Scenario 1 — Post-Meeting Follow-Up Email

**Context:** April 23, 2026, 9:14 AM. Sarah Chen needs to send Robert and Mary a recap email the day after their April 22 review. She has 20 minutes between client meetings.

**The task:** Draft a follow-up email that thanks them, summarizes what was discussed, and lays out next steps. The bar isn't high — but the higher the bar, the wider the gap.

---

### Output 1 — Jump

> _Jump's "Follow-Up Email" generator runs against the meeting transcript and CRM contact record and produces a draft for advisor review. Jump's [March 2026 release notes](https://jump.ai/blog/march-2026-product-update) describe the AI Associate as drafting emails and follow-up tasks with approval gating._

**Context fed to LLM:**
- `meeting_transcript_2026-04-22.txt` (full transcript, 58 min)
- CRM contact card for Smith household (last 5 CRM notes)
- Open action items from CRM (4 items carried over from Q1 review)
- [NOT in context]: any custodial statement, tax return, RSU grant agreement, insurance illustration, estate document, or 529 statement — none of the 24 client vault documents are pulled into this prompt

```
From: Sarah Chen <sarah@beechwoodwealth.com>
To: Robert Smith <robert.smith@megacorp.com>; Mary Smith <mary@hartwellklein.com>
Subject: Following up from yesterday's review

Hi Robert and Mary,

Thank you both for taking the time to meet yesterday — always a pleasure to
catch up with you. A quick recap of what we covered and the next steps I'm
tracking on my end:

What we discussed
- Robert's updated thinking on retirement timing (targeting 60 instead of 62)
- Emma's upcoming start at Carnegie Mellon in the fall and college funding
- Liam's gap year plans before engineering school
- Brief update on the Klein Trust administration — sounds like David has it
  in good hands
- Cape Cod house — re-shingle quotes coming in

Next steps from me
- Pull together a refreshed retirement projection reflecting the 60 target
- Confirm 529 distribution mechanics for Emma's fall semester
- Circle back on Liam's gap year and how 529 rules apply
- Check in next month on the Klein Trust admin progress

Robert — sorry to hear about your dad. Sending best wishes to your family.

Let me know if I missed anything. I'll have the retirement projection ready
for our June 18 quarterly review.

Best,
Sarah Chen, CFP®
Beechwood Wealth Partners
```

**What it does well:** Captures the meeting accurately. Acknowledges the dad reference humanely. Clean structure. Looks professional.

**What it misses:** Every single document-grounded fact. There is no number anywhere in this email. It does not know that Mary's 401(k) lists Edward Klein as primary beneficiary. It does not know about the May 15 RSU vest. It does not know the 529 balances. It does not know the whole-life policy is underperforming. It is, exactly, a competent transcription of what was said, and nothing more.

### Output 2 — Zocks

> _Zocks generates structured action-item-driven emails from its no-recording capture per its [features page](https://www.zocks.io/features) and emphasizes structured note generation + email reply automation in its top tier._

**Context fed to LLM:**
- `meeting_transcript_2026-04-22.txt` (full transcript, parsed to Zocks structured-summary schema)
- Zocks intake form (15 typed fields: household composition, stated retirement age, risk tolerance, etc. — beneficiary slot for Mary's Vanguard 401k is currently blank because nobody typed it)

- [NOT in context]: any custodial statement, tax return, RSU grant agreement, insurance illustration, estate document, or 529 statement — same blind spot as Jump

**What it does well:** Cleaner than Jump on structure. Action items split between advisor-side and client-side is a real Zocks pattern. Compliance-friendly tone.

**What it misses:** Same gap as Jump. Zero document context. The "Robert's father's diagnosis" line is acknowledged but not connected to anything actionable. No specific numbers. No flag on the Vanguard beneficiary problem (because the Vanguard statement isn't in Zocks's universe).

### Output 4 — Manual + ChatGPT

> _The advisor's actual current workflow at most independent RIAs: paste meeting notes into ChatGPT, ask for a recap email, lightly edit, send._

**The advisor's prompt to ChatGPT:**
> "Draft a friendly follow-up email for clients Robert and Mary Smith. We talked about Robert wanting to retire at 60, their daughter Emma going to Carnegie Mellon, their son Liam doing a gap year, and Mary's dad's estate (Klein Trust) being administered. Robert mentioned his dad has Parkinson's. Make it warm but professional."

**Context fed to LLM:**
- Sarah's manual notes from yesterday's call (whatever she remembers — 6 bullet points typed on her phone)
- 1-paragraph CRM note she copy-pasted into the prompt
- Sarah's own framing prompt (above)
- [Sarah forgot to include]: Mary's Vanguard 401k beneficiary issue (she has not opened that file since 2024) / Robert's MassMutual whole-life cash value tracking / Klein Trust K-1 timing detail / RSU vest tax detail (May 15 is three weeks away and she has not modeled it) / 529 balances / Cape Cod re-shingle context. The bottleneck is human assembly — even with ChatGPT in the loop, the prompt only contains what Sarah remembered to type at 9:14 AM with 20 minutes between meetings.

**What it does well:** Warmer voice than the SaaS tools. The advisor's instinct to flag the 2018 estate plan is genuinely good. ChatGPT's prose quality is excellent.

**What it misses:** The estate-plan flag was a guess from Sarah's intuition, not a fact. Sarah knows the 2018 docs are stale — but ChatGPT doesn't know what's *in* them, doesn't know the trust holds the Cape Cod house, doesn't know the will predates Klein and Charles. No numbers. No 529 balance. No RSU vest. No beneficiary problem. No whole-life issue. The depth ceiling is whatever Sarah remembers to type into the prompt. **And she has 20 minutes.**
